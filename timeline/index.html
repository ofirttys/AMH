Market & Policy Chronology · 1983–2026

The Ontario Fertility Timeline

How Ontario's IVF market moved from a de-insured, hospital-based service to a publicly co-funded industry now being consolidated by private equity — clinics, funding, and the pandemic year that accelerated it all. Includes a Mount Sinai Fertility thread woven through in plum.

1983–2010 Before public funding

IVF in Ontario starts inside teaching hospitals, gets de-insured in the '90s, and slowly spins out into independent, physician-owned clinics.

1983Clinic

Toronto East General records Canada's first confirmed IVF pregnancy

Toronto East General Hospital (now Michael Garron Hospital) recorded Canada's first confirmed IVF pregnancy the same year a UBC patient delivered the country's first IVF birth, on Christmas Day 1983 — the starting point for organized IVF practice in Ontario.

Apr 1994Policy

Ontario de-lists IVF from OHIP

The province removes general IVF coverage from public health insurance, keeping a narrow exception only for complete bilateral fallopian tube blockage. IVF becomes almost entirely a private-pay service for the next two decades.

c. 2005–2010Clinic

The "extrication wave": clinics spin out of hospitals

Programs originally embedded in institutions like Mount Sinai and the Ottawa Civic begin separating into independent, physician-owned practices for operational flexibility — the structural shift that later makes the sector acquirable.

UndatedMSF

MSF was formerly known under a different name

Before Dr. Robert Casper left to found TRIO Fertility (with Dr. Ken Cadesky and Dr. Carl Laskin), Mount Sinai's fertility was operated under a different name - "The Centre for Ferility and Reproductive Health." Exact former name and date unconfirmed in public sources.

2009Policy

Expert panel recommends public IVF funding

A provincial expert panel, including Dr. Ellen Greenblatt, recommends Ontario significantly expand IVF funding and mandate single-embryo transfer, citing cost as the primary barrier to care — the blueprint the government adopts six years later.

2010MSF

Gynecologic REI recognized as a national subspecialty

The Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada (RCPSC) formally recognizes Gynecologic REI as an official subspecialty. Mount Sinai's program is among the foundational programs standardized under these national guidelines. [source]

2015–2019 The Ontario Fertility Program launches

Public funding returns for the first time in over 20 years — and immediately runs into demand it wasn't sized for.

Apr 2015Policy

Wynne government announces publicly funded IVF

Health Minister Eric Hoskins announces roughly $50 million a year in new funding for one publicly funded IVF cycle plus unlimited funded IUI, for OHIP-eligible people under 43 — the policy that becomes the Ontario Fertility Program (OFP). Funding comes from Ontario Health.

Oct 2015Policy

Ontario names the 50-clinic funded network

The province details the network of roughly 50 participating clinics and hospitals expected to deliver the program, alongside the mandatory single-embryo-transfer rule designed to cut costly multiple-birth complications.

Dec 2015Policy

Ontario Fertility Program formally opens

Funded cycles begin province-wide. It is the first general public IVF funding in Ontario since the 1994 delisting.

2016Clinic

Wait lists balloon; some clinics turn to a lottery

Demand for funded cycles far exceeds capacity, producing wait lists reported as long as three years. Several clinics adopt lottery-style systems to allocate limited funded slots fairly among patients.

Oct 2016Clinic

First OFP-funded babies are born

The first births conceived through publicly funded cycles arrive, roughly ten months after the program opened.

Dec 2016Policy

OFP's first anniversary: 6,500+ people funded

The province marks one year of the program, reporting more than 6,500 people funded to date and projecting up to $500M in avoided health costs over a decade from fewer multiple-birth complications under the single-embryo-transfer rule.

2016Clinic

Anova Fertility & Reproductive Health founded

Dr. Marjorie Dixon founds Anova in Toronto. It grows into one of the GTA's larger independent clinics before taking private-equity backing three years later.

~ 2017MSF

MSF site relocation discussions finalized (first time)

Leadership considers relocating the clinic and decides to postpone: the lease is extended and the 4th floor is renovated instead.

Jun 2019Policy

Federal surrogate/donor reimbursement rules take effect

Health Canada's Reimbursement Related to Assisted Human Reproduction Regulations come into force, finally clarifying which surrogate and donor expenses can legally be reimbursed under the 2004 Assisted Human Reproduction Act — directly shaping third-party family building through Ontario clinics.

Jun 27, 2019Ownership

Anova takes on its first private-equity partner

Persistence Capital Partners, a Canadian healthcare-focused PE firm, invests in Anova to fund new clinics and expansion — an early signal of institutional capital moving into Ontario fertility care.

~ 2019MSF

Mark Evans departs as Managing Director

Evans, MSF's Managing Director, leaves. He later takes the same role at Markham Fertility Centre.

2020–2021 COVID-19, then the roll-up begins

The pandemic forces a province-wide shutdown of fertility care — and the same year, the first national private-equity platform launches out of Toronto.

Feb 25, 2020COVID-19

CFAS issues its first COVID-19 advisory

The Canadian Fertility & Andrology Society advises caution around non-essential travel and flags that pregnancy increases susceptibility to respiratory infection — the first in a long run of pandemic guidance to Canadian clinics.

Mar 18, 2020COVID-19

Fertility treatment formally suspended

CFAS directs clinics to move consultations to telemedicine and suspend all diagnostic and elective procedures, pausing new cycle starts except for urgent oncology fertility preservation. Ontario clinics, including Mount Sinai Fertility, halt active treatment.

Apr–May 2020COVID-19

CFAS publishes a phased-reopening framework

Guidance shifts from suspension to restart planning on April 15, then a detailed resumption framework follows on May 13, once local safety criteria can be met.

May–Jun 2020COVID-19

Ontario clinics begin phased reopening

Clinics across the province resume treatment in stages as case counts ease, with CFAS endorsing an ASRM/ESHRE/IFFS joint statement in June affirming reproductive care as essential healthcare.

~2020MSF

Dr. Rhonda Zwingerman departs

Dr. Rhonda Zwingerman leaves Mount Sinai Fertility to establish an independent clinic called Twig.

~2020MSF

Dr. Shannon Moore departs

Dr. Shannon Moore leaves Mount Sinai Fertility; she is now a physician at Hannam Fertility Centre.

Jul 3, 2020Ownership

The Fertility Partners launches with $90M in PE backing

Backed by Peloton Capital Management and Heal Partners, TFP launches as a national consolidation platform, acquiring seven founding clinics — including Procrea's Vaughan and Newmarket locations and Mississauga's Reproductive Care Centre. It's the start of large-scale private-equity roll-up in Canadian fertility care.

2021Ownership

TRIO Fertility acquired by Spain's Eugin Group

TRIO, one of Toronto's largest and best-known independent clinics, is acquired by the PE-backed Eugin Group — its first change of ownership, and not its last.

2021MSF

Dr. Rebecca Arthur departs for Hannam

After more than a decade on staff — including leading Quality Improvement for the REI division — Dr. Rebecca Arthur leaves for Hannam Fertility Centre.

2021MSF

Eileen McMahon, NP, departs for Hannam

Nurse practitioner Eileen McMahon leaves for Hannam Fertility Centre; a past president of CFAS, the only non-physician in this role.

Jul 19, 2021Clinic

Procrea Vaughan & Newmarket rebrand as Generation Fertility

The Fertility Partners renames its Vaughan and Newmarket clinics — operating as Procrea since 2011 — to Generation Fertility, alongside the reopening of a larger, renovated Newmarket location.

Jul 2021MSF

Dr. Crystal Chan departs for Markham Fertility Centre

Dr. Chan leaves Mount Sinai Fertility to become Scientific Director at Markham Fertility Centre, newly independent following its physician-led ownership change the same month.

Jul 27, 2021Ownership

Markham Fertility Centre changes hands — but stays physician-owned

Founder Dr. Michael Virro sells Markham Fertility Centre to three of his own physician leaders — Dr. Meivys Garcia (Medical Director), Dr. Marta Wais (Quality Director), and Dr. Crystal Chan (Scientific Director, joining from Mount Sinai Fertility). A rare case of an Ontario clinic changing hands through physician succession rather than a PE or network acquisition.

2022–2024 The consolidation wave

A dense run of acquisitions reshapes GTA and Ontario ownership, even as new venture-backed challenger clinics try to open alongside the roll-ups.

Feb 7, 2022Ownership

Fertility Partners acquires Ottawa Fertility Centre

TFP extends its Ontario footprint east, folding one of the province's larger academically-affiliated clinics into its network.

Mar 8, 2022Clinic

EVOLVE opens as Canada's first egg-freezing-only clinic

Founded by CEO Nicole Condon and operating under the TRIO Fertility family, EVOLVE launches in Toronto as the country's first clinic dedicated solely to egg freezing, citing rising demand for fertility preservation — including a pandemic-era jump in interest.

Mar 22, 2022Clinic

Twig Fertility opens its flagship Toronto clinic

Twig opens a full IVF and egg-freezing clinic and lab in Midtown Toronto (313 Eglinton Ave. W) — the first location for what becomes a fast-growing, tech-forward challenger to the established GTA players.

2022Ownership

Fertility Partners acquires KARMA (Kitchener)

Kitchener Area Reproductive Medicine Associates joins the TFP network, extending the platform into southwestern Ontario.

2022Ownership

Fertility Partners acquires TIRM and sperm bank ReproMed

The Toronto Institute for Reproductive Medicine and its affiliated sperm bank, ReproMed, join TFP — by this point TFP operates seven Ontario locations among 13 IVF centres nationally.

2022Ownership

CCRM Fertility (US) acquires Hannam Fertility Centre

Denver-based CCRM Fertility enters Toronto by acquiring Dr. Tom Hannam's downtown clinic, rebranding it CCRM Fertility Toronto — the first major US network to establish a direct Ontario presence.

Jan 2023MSF

Dr. Vanessa Bacal joins Mount Sinai Fertility

Dr. Bacal joins as Clinician-Investigator and Assistant Professor, with a focus on recurrent pregnancy loss and reproductive outcomes.

Apr 2023MSF

Dr. Nigel Pereira joins Mount Sinai Fertility

Dr. Pereira joins as Clinician-Investigator and Associate Professor. He later supervises Canada's first birth following a uterine transplant (reported Aug 2026).

Jun 2023Ownership

Twig Fertility raises an $8M Series A

Rhino Ventures leads an $8-million round into Twig, a tech-forward Toronto clinic — venture capital, not just PE roll-up money, entering the sector.

2023Clinic

Anova opens a second downtown Toronto location

Anova expands its physical footprint in the city with a new downtown clinic, still independent of the PE roll-up platforms at this stage.

2023Clinic

Pollin Fertility opens in Toronto with $20M backing

A new 20,000 sq. ft. flagship clinic opens at 2360 Yonge St. (Yonge & Eglinton), backed by $20 million from David Thomson's Osmington Inc. — another well-capitalized new entrant competing against the consolidating incumbents.

2023Ownership

Vitrolife acquires Igenomix for ~$2.8B

A global consolidation deal in genetic testing and embryo screening — infrastructure that Ontario IVF labs, including those doing PGT, rely on — signalling the roll-up extending past clinics into the supply chain.

2023MSF

MSF site relocation discussions restart

The question of relocating the clinic is revisited, six years after being settled the first time.

Oct 2023MSF

Dr. Jennia Michaeli joins Mount Sinai Fertility

Dr. Michaeli joins as Clinician-Investigator and Assistant Professor, with a research focus on oncofertility and fertility preservation.

Nov 2023MSF

MSF establishes its OTC program

Mount Sinai Fertility establishes its ovarian tissue cryopreservation (OTC) program — fertility preservation for patients facing treatment that threatens their fertility. The program's team later leads Canada's first published ovarian tissue transport case (CMAJ, Nov 2025).

Jan 31, 2024Ownership

IVI RMA completes $535M purchase of Eugin's North American arm

Spain's IVI RMA (backed by KKR) completes a $535-million acquisition of Eugin Group's Boston IVF network and TRIO Fertility — TRIO's second change of ownership in three years.

2024Clinic

Market snapshot: 43 clinics, ~7,700 private cycles a year

Ontario now counts roughly 43 fertility clinics performing more than 7,700 privately funded IVF cycles annually (over $150M in private spend) — with close to a third of clinics tied to investor-backed networks rather than independent physician owners.

c. 2024–2025Clinic

Twig opens a second Toronto clinic, downtown

Twig adds a downtown, PATH-connected location at 130 King St. W — its first expansion beyond the Midtown flagship.

2024–2026 Funding expands, challengers scale up

A decade after the OFP first launched, Queen's Park returns with its largest funding increases yet, naming clinics directly — while Twig and Pollin turn Toronto footholds into multi-city networks.

Oct 28, 2024Policy

Ontario commits $150M to triple funded families

The province announces $150 million over two years to expand the OFP through hospitals and community clinics, aiming to roughly triple the number of families accessing government-funded fertility services — plus a new personal fertility tax credit for January 2025.

Jan 2025Policy

Ontario Fertility Tax Credit takes effect

Residents can now claim 25% of eligible fertility expenses — IVF cycles, medications, diagnostic testing, and related travel — up to $5,000 per year, backed by $115 million over three years.

Mar 17, 2025Clinic

Generation Fertility expands to Toronto West

The Fertility Partners brand opens a Toronto West location — the former ReproMed Fertility site at 56 Aberfoyle Cres. in Etobicoke, near Islington Station — led by Dr. Violetta Buduryan.

Late 2025MSF

MSF site relocation discussions finalized again

For the second time since 2017, the relocation question is settled: postpone again, extend the lease to 2032, and renovate the 7th and 4th floors.

Oct 8, 2025Policy

$250M expansion adds Pollin, Twig & Tripod to the OFP

A further $250M ($50M for 2025/26, $200M through 2027/28) brings three previously private-pay GTA clinics — Pollin, Twig, and Tripod Fertility — onto the publicly funded roster for the first time. TRIO, Generation Fertility Toronto West, CReATe, Mount Sinai Fertility, and Hannam all receive expanded funded volumes. The eight Toronto clinics are expected to deliver roughly 2,250 IVF cycles combined; the OFP now spans 54 clinics province-wide.

2025–2026Clinic

Pollin opens a Sudbury clinic

Pollin extends outside the GTA for the first time, operating through the Neo Women's Health Network in Sudbury — part of the same expansion wave following its OFP funding. Exact opening date not confirmed in public sources.

2025Ownership

IVI RMA keeps consolidating across North America

Fresh acquisitions elsewhere in IVI RMA's North American network underline that the roll-up pressure on independent clinics — the kind that reshaped TRIO and Hannam — is still active going into 2026.

Mar 30, 2026Clinic

Twig opens its first clinic outside Ontario, in Vancouver

Twig's third location — after Midtown and Downtown Toronto — opens in Vancouver, its first outside Ontario, citing long wait times and a shortage of specialists and lab capacity in B.C.

Apr 28, 2026Policy

A further $100M pushes the OFP to 56 clinics

Ontario allocates another $100 million across 27 clinics — including Halton Fertility and Women's Health Centre in Oakville — expected to fund roughly 10,000 additional IVF cycles this year. The OFP now supports 56 clinics province-wide, and Health Minister Sylvia Jones says the program has helped nearly 133,000 Ontarians build their families since its 2015 launch.

Jun 19, 2026Clinic

Pollin opens in Ottawa (phase one)

Consultations, diagnostic testing, cycle monitoring, and low-intervention treatment begin at Pollin's new Ottawa clinic; the embryology lab — enabling egg retrievals, transfers, and cryopreservation on-site — is expected to follow in late fall 2026.

2026Ownership

Physician ownership falls to roughly 30% of the market

A decade after independent physician-owners controlled 70–80% of Canadian fertility practices, that share has fallen to roughly 30% — the rest split between private-equity platforms, partnerships, and larger conglomerates.
